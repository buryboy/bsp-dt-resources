# Cams, gears & pulleys — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.5 Mechanical devices: rotary systems)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Follows the "Levers & linkages" workbook (students know the four types of motion: linear, reciprocating, rotary, oscillating; input/output; mechanical advantage as "more force but less movement"). Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth only.

## 1. Rotary systems
- Rotary systems change the **size (magnitude) and direction of forces and motion**. AQA names three: **cams and followers, simple gear trains, pulleys and belts**.
- Speed of rotation is measured in **rpm** (revolutions per minute). **Torque** = turning force.
- Trade-off (never something for nothing): **slower output = more torque; faster output = less torque.**

## 2. Cams and followers
- A **cam** is a specially shaped piece fixed to a rotating shaft. A **follower** rests on the cam's edge and is held in a **guide** (slide) so it can only move up and down.
- A cam and follower changes **rotary motion into reciprocating motion** (the cam turns; the follower goes up and down). It does not work the other way round (the follower cannot drive the cam).
- Words: **rise** (follower moves up), **fall** (moves down), **dwell** (follower stays still while the cam still turns), **stroke / lift** (how far the follower moves from lowest to highest).
- One full turn of the cam = one full cycle of the follower's movement.
### Cam shapes
- **Eccentric (circular / off-centre) cam**: a circle with the shaft off-centre. Follower rises and falls **smoothly and continuously, no dwell**. Used in pumps (e.g. fuel pump), steam engines.
- **Pear cam**: pear/egg shape. Follower **dwells for about half the turn**, then rises and falls smoothly (symmetrical). Used for opening and closing valves in car engines (the camshaft).
- **Snail (drop) cam**: spiral like a snail shell. Follower **rises slowly and steadily then drops suddenly**. Must only turn **one way** (the other way it jams). Used in hammers/stamping machines, striking mechanisms, and toys where something drops or snaps.
- (Mention only) heart-shaped cam: rises and falls at constant speed, no dwell (winding thread evenly on a bobbin).
### Follower types
- **Flat follower**: flat bottom; copes with side load, but more friction; cannot follow dips.
- **Knife (point) follower**: pointed; follows the cam shape most accurately, but wears quickly.
- **Roller follower**: has a small wheel; least friction and wear, runs smoothly at speed; more expensive/complex.
- Uses of cams: car engine valves (camshaft), mechanical toys / automata, sewing machines, pumps, old machine tools.

## 3. Simple gear trains
- A **gear** is a wheel with **teeth** around its edge. Teeth **mesh** (interlock) so gears cannot slip. Two or more meshed gears = a **gear train**. The standard straight-toothed gear is a **spur gear**.
- **Driver gear** (input, turned by the motor/effort) and **driven gear** (output).
- Two meshed gears turn in **opposite directions**.
- An **idler gear** between driver and driven makes them turn in the **same direction**. The idler does **not** change the gear ratio/speed.
- **Small driver → big driven: output is SLOWER but has MORE torque** (gearing down; e.g. low gear on a bike for hills, a winch, a food mixer).
- **Big driver → small driven: output is FASTER but has LESS torque** (gearing up; e.g. hand whisk, top gear on a bike).
- **Gear ratio = number of teeth on DRIVEN gear ÷ number of teeth on DRIVER gear.** Written like 3:1 (say "three to one"). Example: driver 20 teeth, driven 60 teeth → 60 ÷ 20 = 3 → 3:1: the driver turns 3 times for every 1 turn of the driven.
- **Output speed = input speed ÷ gear ratio.** Example: input 300 rpm, ratio 3:1 → 300 ÷ 3 = 100 rpm.
- Gearing up example: driver 40 teeth, driven 10 teeth → 10 ÷ 40 = 0.25 (1:4). Input 100 rpm → 100 ÷ 0.25 = 400 rpm.
- (Mention only, other gear types) **bevel gears**: turn the drive through 90° (hand drill); **worm and worm wheel**: very large speed reduction, 90°, the wheel cannot drive the worm (guitar tuning pegs); **rack and pinion**: rotary ↔ linear (pillar drill handle, steering, canal lock gates).
- Uses: clocks and watches, bicycles (with chain and sprockets: same idea, same direction), car gearboxes, hand whisks, drills.

## 4. Pulleys and belts
- A **pulley** is a wheel with a groove; a **belt** runs in the grooves and links a **driver pulley** to a **driven pulley** some distance apart.
- Both pulleys turn in the **same direction**. A **crossed belt** (figure of 8) makes them turn in **opposite directions**.
- **Small driver → big driven: slower, more torque. Big driver → small driven: faster, less torque** (same rule as gears).
- **Velocity ratio = diameter of DRIVEN pulley ÷ diameter of DRIVER pulley.** Example: driver 40 mm, driven 120 mm → 120 ÷ 40 = 3 (3:1).
- **Output speed = input speed ÷ velocity ratio** (= input speed × driver diameter ÷ driven diameter). Example: 1500 rpm ÷ 3 = 500 rpm.
- Belts work by **friction**, so they can **slip**. Slip is useful as protection (if a machine jams, the belt slips instead of the motor burning out) but means the drive is not exact. A **toothed belt** (with a toothed pulley) cannot slip (car timing belt, 3D printers).
- **Belts vs gears**: belts are quiet, cheap, need no lubrication, link shafts far apart, absorb shock, but can slip, stretch and wear. Gears never slip (exact ratio), carry big forces, are compact, but are noisier, need lubrication, cost more, and shafts must be close.
- A **stepped cone pulley** (several pulley sizes on each shaft) lets you change speed by moving the belt: used on a **pillar drill** (big drill bits / hard metals need a slow speed; small bits / wood need a fast speed).
- Uses: pillar drill, washing machine drum, car fan/alternator belt, sewing machine, lathe, vacuum cleaner brush.
### Lifting pulleys (brief)
- A **single fixed pulley** only changes the **direction** of the force (pull down to lift up): effort = load, MA = 1 (flagpole, window blind).
- Adding **one moving pulley** (two ropes holding the load) **halves the effort**: MA = 2, but you pull **twice as much rope** (block and tackle on cranes, engine hoists, sailing boats).

## 5. Products (for detective/application)
| Product | Mechanism | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Car engine valves | pear cams on a camshaft | rotary → reciprocating, with dwell (valve stays shut) |
| Automaton / mechanical toy | cams + followers | figures bob up and down |
| Stamping hammer toy | snail cam | slow lift, sudden drop |
| Hand whisk | big driver gear, small driven gears | gears up: beaters spin faster |
| Bicycle low gear | small front sprocket, big rear | slower wheel, more torque for hills |
| Clock | gear train | slows rotation exactly, no slip |
| Pillar drill | stepped cone pulleys + belt | change spindle speed |
| Washing machine | small motor pulley, big drum pulley, belt | slows drum, more torque, quiet, belt can slip |
| Car timing belt | toothed belt | no slip, exact timing |
| Flagpole | single fixed pulley | changes direction only |
| Crane hook block | moving pulleys | multiplies force |

## Misconception tags (use these exact ids)
- `cam-motion` — thinks a cam gives rotary/oscillating output, or that the follower drives the cam
- `cam-shape` — mixes up pear, eccentric and snail cam movements
- `dwell-meaning` — thinks dwell means the cam stops, or means the fall
- `follower-type` — mixes up flat, knife and roller followers
- `gear-direction` — thinks meshed gears turn the same way
- `idler-ratio` — thinks an idler changes the speed/ratio (or doesn't know it restores direction)
- `ratio-upside-down` — calculates driver ÷ driven
- `speed-multiply` — multiplies by the ratio instead of dividing (or vice versa)
- `big-is-fast` — thinks a bigger driven gear/pulley turns faster
- `speed-torque` — thinks you can get more speed AND more torque
- `belt-direction` — thinks a plain belt reverses direction / crossed belt keeps it
- `belt-slip` — thinks belts never slip, or that slip is always bad, or that gears slip
- `belt-vs-gear` — picks the wrong drive for the job
- `pulley-lift` — thinks a single fixed pulley reduces the effort
