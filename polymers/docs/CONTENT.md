# Polymers — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.6 Materials and their working properties: polymers)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Students have done the Material Properties workbook. Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth: no chemistry formulae.

## 1. Where polymers come from
- Most polymers (plastics) are **synthetic**, made from **crude oil**, a **finite (non-renewable) fossil fuel**. (Some natural polymers exist: rubber/latex, cellulose. **Bio-polymers** such as PLA are made from plants like corn starch and are biodegradable.)
- Steps: crude oil is drilled → **fractional distillation** at a refinery separates it into fractions (one is **naphtha**) → **cracking** breaks naphtha into small molecules called **monomers** (e.g. ethene) → **polymerisation** joins thousands of monomers into long chains called **polymers** ("poly" = many; "mono" = one).
- Additives are mixed in: **pigments** (colour), **plasticisers** (make it softer/more flexible), **stabilisers** (resist UV sunlight), fillers, flame retardants.
- Polymers are sold as granules/pellets, powders, sheets, rods, films, foams.

## 2. Two families
- **Thermoforming polymers (thermoplastics)**: **soften when heated and can be shaped; harden when cooled; and this can be repeated** again and again. Their long chains are tangled but **not joined to each other (no cross-links)**, so heat lets the chains slide. They can be **recycled** by melting. They have "plastic memory": reheated, a formed sheet tries to return to flat.
- **Thermosetting polymers (thermosets)**: are shaped **once**; heat (or a chemical hardener) makes the chains form strong **cross-links**, setting them permanently. **They cannot be softened or reshaped by heating** (they char/burn instead). Usually **hard, rigid, brittle, heat-resistant and good electrical insulators**. They **cannot be recycled by melting**.
- General polymer properties: lightweight, waterproof, do not rot or corrode, electrical and thermal insulators, easy to mould into complex shapes, can be any colour, cheap in large numbers.

## 3. Thermoforming polymers (AQA list)
| Polymer | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Acrylic (PMMA)** | hard, stiff, shiny, can be transparent (glass-like) or brightly coloured, weather-resistant; **brittle and scratches easily** | shop signs, display stands, baths, car light lenses, aquarium/aeroplane windows, school laser-cutting |
| **High impact polystyrene (HIPS)** | tough (impact-resistant), lightweight, fairly rigid, cheap, **very easy to vacuum form**, food safe | yoghurt pots, vacuum-formed packaging trays, toy and electronics casings, fridge linings |
| **High density polythene (HDPE)** | stiff, strong, tough, chemical-resistant, waxy feel, opaque | milk bottles, bleach/detergent bottles, pipes, buckets, wheelie bins, chopping boards |
| **Polypropylene (PP)** | tough, flexible, **resists fatigue (can bend many times: "living hinge")**, chemical- and heat-resistant, lightweight | lunch-box and flip-top lids with hinges, school chairs, crates, bottle caps, syringes, rope, food containers (microwave safe) |
| **Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)** | hard and tough; **rigid (uPVC) or made flexible with plasticiser**; weather- and chemical-resistant; good electrical insulator | window frames, guttering and drain pipes, electrical cable insulation, blister packs, raincoats, hose pipes |
| **Polyethylene terephthalate (PET)** | clear, tough, strong, lightweight, food safe, keeps fizz in (good barrier) | fizzy drinks and water bottles, food trays, polyester fibre for clothing |

## 4. Thermosetting polymers (AQA list)
| Polymer | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Epoxy resin (ER)** | mixed from resin + hardener; very strong adhesive, hard, electrical insulator, chemical- and heat-resistant; brittle unless reinforced | strong two-part adhesives, printed circuit boards, coating/encapsulating electronics, bonding carbon fibre |
| **Melamine formaldehyde (MF)** | hard, scratch-, heat- and stain-resistant, food safe, takes bright colours | kitchen worktop laminates, picnic/children's tableware |
| **Phenol formaldehyde (PF)** | hard, brittle, very heat-resistant, good electrical insulator, dark colours only | saucepan handles and knobs, older electrical fittings, snooker balls |
| **Polyester resin (PR)** | liquid resin set with a catalyst; hard, stiff, brittle alone; **reinforced with glass fibre = GRP (fibreglass)**: strong and light | boat hulls, car body panels, garden ponds, casting/encapsulation |
| **Urea formaldehyde (UF)** | hard, stiff, brittle, heat-resistant, good electrical insulator, white/pale colours | electrical plugs, sockets and switches, toilet seats, the adhesive in MDF and plywood |

## 5. Environment
- From finite crude oil; making them uses energy and releases CO2.
- Most are **not biodegradable**: they last hundreds of years in landfill, litter oceans, break into **microplastics**.
- **Thermoforming polymers can be recycled**: collected, sorted by type, shredded, melted, made into new products. Recycling codes (number in a triangle of arrows): **1 PET, 2 HDPE, 3 PVC, 4 LDPE, 5 PP, 6 PS (polystyrene), 7 other**.
- Thermosets cannot be remelted (may be ground up as filler).
- Reduce (less packaging), reuse (refill bottles), recycle; bio-polymers; design for disassembly (label parts with the code).

## Misconception tags (use these exact ids)
- `thermo-swap` — mixes up thermoforming and thermosetting (which softens, which sets)
- `crosslink` — thinks thermoforming polymers have cross-links / thermosets do not
- `thermoset-recycle` — thinks thermosets can be melted and recycled
- `polymer-source` — thinks polymers are mined/grown, or mixes up distillation, cracking and polymerisation
- `monomer-polymer` — mixes up monomer and polymer
- `oil-renewable` — thinks crude oil is renewable / plastics biodegrade quickly
- `thermoform-mixup` — mixes up acrylic, HIPS, HDPE, PP, PVC, PET properties/uses
- `thermoset-mixup` — mixes up ER, MF, PF, PR, UF properties/uses
- `acrylic-tough` — thinks acrylic is tough/impact-resistant (it is brittle)
- `pp-hinge` — doesn't know polypropylene is the hinge polymer
- `code-mixup` — wrong recycling code
- `additive-mixup` — mixes up pigment, plasticiser, stabiliser
- `property-word` — uses the wrong property word (hard vs tough, etc.)
