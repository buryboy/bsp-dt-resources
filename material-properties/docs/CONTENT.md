# Material properties — AQA GCSE D&T (8552), Year 10, one 60-minute lesson

Single source of truth for every page. Only teach/test what is here. UK spelling. Reading age ~13–14: short sentences.

## The two families (AQA wording)

- **Physical properties** describe what a material *is like* (its make-up), whatever you do to it: **absorbency**, **density**, **fusibility**, **electrical conductivity**, **thermal conductivity**.
- **Working properties** (sometimes called mechanical properties) describe how a material *behaves when a force is applied or it is worked*: **strength**, **hardness**, **toughness**, **malleability**, **ductility**, **elasticity**.

## Physical properties

| Property | Definition (learn this) | High example | Low example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absorbency** | how well a material soaks up moisture / liquid (AQA: resistance to moisture) | paper, cotton, untreated softwood, MDF edges | acrylic, glass, metals, polypropylene |
| **Density** | mass per unit volume (how heavy it is for its size); density = mass ÷ volume, g/cm³ | lead 11.3, copper 8.96, steel 7.85, aluminium 2.7 | cork 0.24, pine ≈0.5, polypropylene 0.90 |
| **Fusibility** | the ability of a material to be changed into a liquid (molten) state when heated to its melting point, so it can be joined or cast | solder (melts ≈183–220 °C), aluminium (660 °C), most thermoforming polymers | thermosetting polymers don't melt (they char); wood burns rather than melts |
| **Electrical conductivity** | how well a material lets electricity flow through it | copper, aluminium, steel, all metals | polymers, wood, glass, ceramics = **insulators** |
| **Thermal conductivity** | how well a material lets heat travel through it | copper > aluminium > steel | wood, polymers, glass, air = **thermal insulators** |

Densities to use (g/cm³; water = 1.00, < 1 floats, > 1 sinks): cork 0.24 · balsa 0.16 · pine 0.50 · oak 0.75 · polypropylene (PP) 0.90 · HDPE 0.95 · water 1.00 · ebony 1.12 · acrylic 1.18 · PVC 1.40 · glass 2.5 · aluminium 2.70 · steel 7.85 · copper 8.96 · lead 11.3.

Thermal conductivity order (fastest first, for the wax-and-pin race): copper (≈400 W/m·K) → aluminium (≈205) → brass (≈110) → steel (≈50) → glass (≈1) → wood (≈0.15) (polymers ≈0.2, similar to wood).

Melting points (°C): solder ≈183–220 · polypropylene ≈160 · aluminium 660 · copper 1085 · steel ≈1400–1500 · thermosets: none (char/burn).

## Working properties

| Property | Definition (learn this) | Good example | Poor example / opposite |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strength** | the ability to withstand a force without breaking or permanently bending. Forces: **tension** (pulling), **compression** (squashing), **bending**, **torsion** (twisting), **shear** (sliding/cutting) | steel cable (tension), concrete (compression) | paper, expanded polystyrene |
| **Hardness** | the ability to resist **scratching, wear (abrasion) and indentation (denting)** | glass, high-speed steel drill bit, tool steel | lead, softwood, LDPE |
| **Toughness** | the ability to **absorb energy / an impact without breaking or cracking**. The opposite is **brittle** | low-carbon (mild) steel, polypropylene, rubber | glass, cast iron, acrylic = brittle |
| **Malleability** | the ability to be **permanently shaped by compression** (hammered, pressed, rolled) **without cracking** | copper, aluminium, lead, gold | cast iron, glass |
| **Ductility** | the ability to be **stretched (drawn) out into a thin wire** under tension without breaking | copper, aluminium | cast iron, glass |
| **Elasticity** | the ability to **return to its original shape** after the force is removed | rubber, spring steel, LDPE film (a little) | lead, clay, plasticine (stay deformed = **plastic**) |

### Common misconceptions to target (use as distractors and as true/false cards)
1. **Hard = tough.** False: glass is very hard but brittle (not tough).
2. **Strong = hard.** Different: strength is resisting a force without breaking; hardness is resisting scratches/dents.
3. **Malleable vs ductile mixed up.** Malleable = squash/hammer (compression); ductile = stretch into wire (tension).
4. **Dense = strong / heavy objects are dense.** Density is mass for its *size*; a big log is heavy but pine still floats.
5. **Elastic = stretchy.** Elastic means it *returns* to shape; a material can stretch a lot and not return (plastic deformation).
6. **Metal spoons "are cold" / wood "is warm".** Metals feel cold because they conduct heat away from your hand fast (high thermal conductivity) — they're the same temperature as the room.
7. **All metals are magnetic / all materials that conduct heat conduct electricity equally.** Avoid deep claims; keep: all metals conduct electricity and heat; most non-metals don't.
8. **Brittle = weak.** Brittle materials can be very strong in compression (cast iron, concrete) but snap/shatter under impact.
9. **Absorbency only matters for fabrics/paper.** It matters for timber outdoors (rot, swelling) — finishes reduce absorbency.

## Applying it (product → material → property)

| Product | Material | Key property / reason |
|---|---|---|
| Saucepan body | aluminium or stainless steel | high thermal conductivity (heats food quickly); high melting point |
| Saucepan handle | phenol formaldehyde (thermoset) or wood | low thermal conductivity (handle stays cool); thermoset doesn't soften when hot |
| Electrical cable core | copper | high electrical conductivity; ductile (drawn into wire) |
| Cable insulation | PVC | electrical insulator; flexible |
| Drill bit | high-speed steel | hardness (stays sharp, resists wear) |
| Kayak | HDPE / polypropylene | tough (survives impacts); low density (floats) |
| Car body panel | low-carbon steel or aluminium | malleable (pressed into shape); tough |
| Kitchen roll | paper | absorbent |
| Bike tyre / bungee cord | rubber | elastic |
| Garden shed timber treated with preservative/paint | pine | finish reduces absorbency → prevents rot |
| Solder for joining electronics | tin alloy solder | fusible at a low temperature; conducts electricity |
| Shop window / phone screen | toughened glass | hard (scratch-resistant); transparent — but ordinary glass is brittle |
