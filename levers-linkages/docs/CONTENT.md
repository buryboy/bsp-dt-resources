# Motion, levers & linkages — AQA GCSE D&T (8552) 3.1.5 Mechanical devices, Year 10, one 60-minute lesson

Single source of truth for every page of this workbook. Only teach/test what is here. UK spelling. Reading age ~13–14: short sentences.
(Cams, gears and pulleys are a separate workbook: do not teach them here beyond a passing mention.)

## A mechanism
A **mechanism** is a device that changes an **input** motion and force into a different **output** motion and force. It can change the **type** of motion, its **direction**, its **size (distance)** and the **size of the force**.

## The four types of motion
| Motion | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Linear** | moving in a **straight line in one direction** | a train on a straight track, a paper trimmer blade sliding, a conveyor belt carrying a box |
| **Reciprocating** | moving **backwards and forwards in a straight line** | a sewing-machine needle, a jigsaw blade, a piston in an engine, sawing wood |
| **Rotary** | turning **round in a circle** about an axis | a wheel, a drill bit, the hands of a clock, a fan |
| **Oscillating** | **swinging backwards and forwards in an arc** | a pendulum, a swing, windscreen wipers, a metronome |

Common mix-up: **reciprocating** is back-and-forth in a *straight line*; **oscillating** is back-and-forth along a *curve (arc)*.

## Levers
A **lever** is a rigid bar that turns about a fixed point. Three parts:
- **Fulcrum** (or **pivot**): the fixed point the lever turns about.
- **Effort**: the input force you apply.
- **Load**: the output force / the thing being moved (the resistance).

The **order (class)** of a lever depends on which part is **in the middle**. Memory aid: **F – L – E → 1 – 2 – 3** ("**F**ulcrum in the middle = **1**st, **L**oad in the middle = **2**nd, **E**ffort in the middle = **3**rd").

| Order | In the middle | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First order** | **fulcrum** (between effort and load) | changes the **direction** of the force; can multiply force if the effort is further from the fulcrum than the load | see-saw, crowbar, scissors (two levers), pliers, claw hammer pulling a nail, balance scales |
| **Second order** | **load** (between fulcrum and effort) | always **multiplies force** (effort is further from the fulcrum than the load): mechanical advantage **greater than 1** | wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener, door (hinge = fulcrum), garlic press |
| **Third order** | **effort** (between fulcrum and load) | the load moves **further and faster** than the effort, but needs **more effort than the load**: mechanical advantage **less than 1** | tweezers, tongs, fishing rod, the human forearm (elbow = fulcrum), a broom, a spade lifting soil |

## Mechanical advantage (MA)
- **Mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort** (no units: it is a ratio).
- MA **greater than 1** → the lever multiplies your force (easier to lift), but your hand moves **further** than the load. You never get something for nothing.
- MA **less than 1** → you need more effort than the load, but the load moves further/faster (third-order levers).
- Worked example: a wheelbarrow carries a **load of 600 N** and needs an **effort of 200 N**. MA = 600 ÷ 200 = **3**.
- Rearranged: **effort = load ÷ MA**; **load = effort × MA**.
- Force is measured in **newtons (N)**. (On Earth 1 kg weighs about 10 N.)

## Balancing a lever (moments)
- The turning effect of a force is a **moment**: **moment = force × distance from the fulcrum** (N × m = Nm, or N × cm = Ncm).
- A lever **balances** when **effort × its distance = load × its distance**.
- Worked example: a 400 N load sits 0.5 m from the fulcrum of a see-saw. To balance it from 2 m away on the other side: effort × 2 = 400 × 0.5 = 200, so effort = **100 N**.
- So: **the further from the fulcrum you push, the less effort you need** (longer spanner, longer crowbar, door handles far from the hinge).
- For a lever, MA can also be found from distances: MA = distance from fulcrum to effort ÷ distance from fulcrum to load.

## Linkages
A **linkage** is a set of levers (rigid bars) joined by pivots that **transfers motion and force** from an input to an output. Two kinds of pivot:
- **Fixed pivot**: attached to the frame/base; the bar turns about it but it does not move.
- **Moving pivot**: joins two bars together; it moves with them.

| Linkage | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Reverse motion linkage** | output moves in the **opposite direction** to the input (push in → comes out the other way). One lever turning on a **fixed pivot in the middle**. If the fixed pivot is **central**, input and output forces/distances are equal; move the pivot **nearer the output** and the output force is **bigger** but its movement **smaller** (and the other way round). | folding clothes airer, pop-up card mechanisms, some foot-pedal bins |
| **Push–pull linkage** (parallel motion) | output moves in the **same direction** as the input, parallel to it. Uses **two fixed pivots** and three bars (a "Z" shape). | tool-box trays that stay level as they open, moving-picture book tabs |
| **Bell crank linkage** | turns the motion through **90°** (round a corner). An L-shaped lever on a fixed pivot at the corner. | bicycle brake calipers, steering linkages |
| **Crank and slider** | converts **rotary** motion into **reciprocating** motion (or the other way round). A rotating crank, a connecting rod and a slider in a guide. | car engine piston and crankshaft (reciprocating → rotary), a pump, a jigsaw, mechanical toy |
| **Treadle linkage** | converts **oscillating** motion (a foot pedal rocking) into **rotary** motion (or rotary into oscillating). | old sewing machine or potter's wheel treadle; **windscreen wipers** (motor's rotary → oscillating wipers) |
| **Lazy tongs / scissor linkage** | several crossed levers that extend a long way from a small input | scissor lift, extending mirror, grabber toy |

## Applying it (product → mechanism)
| Product | Mechanism / lever | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wheelbarrow | second-order lever | load between wheel (fulcrum) and handles; multiplies force |
| Scissors | pair of first-order levers | fulcrum between hands and blades |
| Tweezers / kitchen tongs | third-order levers | effort in the middle; small, precise, fast movement at the tips |
| Bottle opener | second-order lever | fulcrum on the cap top, load = cap edge, effort at the handle end |
| Crowbar | first-order lever | long effort arm → large mechanical advantage |
| Bicycle brake lever + caliper | levers + bell crank | changes direction of the cable pull through 90° and multiplies force |
| Windscreen wipers | treadle-type linkage | rotary motion of the motor → oscillating wipers |
| Car engine | crank and slider | reciprocating pistons → rotary crankshaft |
| Folding clothes airer | reverse motion linkage | bars move in opposite directions to fold flat |
| Scissor lift | lazy tongs | small input movement → large extension |

## Misconception tags (use these in `mis`)
- `recip-vs-oscill` — mixes up reciprocating (straight line) and oscillating (arc)
- `linear-vs-recip` — thinks any straight-line movement is linear (ignores back-and-forth)
- `lever-order` — mixes up first/second/third order (which part is in the middle)
- `fulcrum-load-effort` — mislabels fulcrum, load or effort
- `ma-upside-down` — calculates MA as effort ÷ load
- `ma-free-lunch` — thinks a lever gives more force *and* more movement (something for nothing)
- `third-order-ma` — thinks third-order levers multiply force
- `moment-distance` — ignores distance from the fulcrum (thinks force alone decides balance)
- `fixed-vs-moving` — mixes up fixed and moving pivots
- `linkage-type` — mixes up reverse-motion, push–pull and bell crank
- `motion-conversion` — wrong input/output motion for crank-and-slider or treadle
