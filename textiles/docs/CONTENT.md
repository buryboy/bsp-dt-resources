# Textiles — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.6 Materials and their working properties: textiles)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Students have done the Material Properties workbook. Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth.

## 1. Fibres → yarns → fabrics
- A **fibre** is a very fine, hair-like strand. Fibres are **spun** (twisted together) into a **yarn** (thread). Yarns are **woven** or **knitted** into a **fabric**; some fabrics are made straight from fibres (**non-woven**).
- **Staple fibres** are short (e.g. cotton, wool); **filament fibres** are very long, continuous (silk, and synthetic fibres as made). Twisting gives yarn strength.

## 2. Natural fibres
- **Plant (cellulose) fibres**: cotton (from the seed pod/boll of the cotton plant), linen (from the stem of the flax plant).
- **Animal (protein) fibres**: wool (from the fleece of sheep), silk (from the cocoon of the silkworm).
- Natural fibres are **renewable** and **biodegradable**; but growing cotton uses a lot of **water** and often pesticides; animal fibres need farming.

| Fibre | Source | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cotton** | cotton plant seed pod | soft, strong (stronger when wet), **absorbent**, cool and comfortable to wear, breathable; creases easily, shrinks, slow to dry, burns easily | jeans (denim), T-shirts, towels, underwear, bedding |
| **Linen** | flax plant stem | very strong, absorbent, **cool** and crisp, fresh to wear in hot weather; **creases very easily**, stiff, can be expensive | summer clothing, tablecloths, tea towels |
| **Wool** | sheep fleece | **warm** (fibres trap air: good insulator), soft, elastic/resilient (creases drop out), absorbent, naturally **flame resistant** (smoulders, doesn't flare); can **shrink and felt** if washed hot, can feel itchy, slow to dry | jumpers, coats, suits, carpets, blankets |
| **Silk** | silkworm cocoon | very smooth, **lustrous** (shiny), soft, drapes well, strong for its weight, absorbent, warm in winter / cool in summer; expensive, weaker when wet, needs careful washing | luxury dresses, ties, scarves, lingerie, wedding dresses |

## 3. Synthetic fibres
- Made from **crude oil** (they are polymers), a finite resource. Most are **thermoplastic**: they can be **heat-set** (pleats stay in) but can **melt** if ironed too hot. Most are **not biodegradable**, but some can be recycled (e.g. polyester from PET bottles). Washing sheds **microfibres** into water.
- Generally strong, durable, crease-resistant, quick drying, low absorbency.

| Fibre | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Polyester** | very strong and **durable**, **crease-resistant**, **quick drying**, low absorbency (not very breathable: can feel sweaty), cheap, heat-sets; often blended with cotton | sportswear, fleece jackets, bedding (polycotton), duvet filling, sails, shirts |
| **Polyamide (nylon)** | very **strong** and **hard-wearing**, elastic, smooth, lightweight, quick drying, low absorbency; melts | tights and stockings, swimwear, rope, parachutes, tents, toothbrush bristles, carpets |
| **Elastane (Lycra)** | **extremely stretchy**: stretches to about 5–7 times its length and springs back; lightweight; added in small amounts (around 2–20%) to other fibres | sportswear, swimwear, leggings, stretch jeans, cycling shorts, underwear |
- (Mention only) **regenerated** fibres such as viscose are made from wood pulp cellulose treated with chemicals.

## 4. Blends and mixtures
- A **blend** = two or more different fibres mixed together **before** spinning into one yarn. A **mixture** = different yarns used in one fabric (e.g. a cotton warp and polyester weft).
- Why: combine the good properties and reduce the bad ones, improve appearance/handle, and reduce cost.
- **Polycotton** (polyester + cotton, e.g. 65/35): comfortable and absorbent like cotton, but stronger, **creases less**, dries faster and is cheaper. Used for school shirts, bedsheets.
- **Cotton + elastane**: stretch jeans (comfortable, keep their shape).
- **Wool + polyamide**: hard-wearing socks and carpets.

## 5. Fabric construction
### Woven fabrics
- Made by **interlacing** two sets of yarns at right angles on a **loom**. **Warp** yarns run **lengthways** (along the fabric, held on the loom); **weft** yarns run **across** (from side to side), passing over and under the warp. The **selvedge** is the firm self-finished edge that **does not fray**, running down both sides parallel to the warp. Woven fabrics **fray** at cut edges and have little stretch (except on the **bias**, at 45°).
- **Plain weave**: weft goes **over one, under one**. Strong, hard-wearing, firm, same both sides, cheap and simple. E.g. calico, cotton shirting, poplin.
- **Twill weave**: weft goes over two (or more), under one, shifting one step each row, giving a **diagonal line** pattern. Strong, hard-wearing, drapes well, hides dirt. E.g. **denim**, gabardine, chino.
- **Satin weave**: weft floats over **four or more** warp yarns. **Smooth, shiny** (lustrous) surface, drapes well; floats can **snag** and are less hard-wearing. E.g. evening wear, linings, ribbons.
### Knitted fabrics
- Made from yarn formed into interlocking **loops**. **Stretchy** (comfortable, good fit), warm (traps air), **crease-resistant**, do not fray easily but can **ladder/unravel**; can lose shape.
- **Weft knit**: one continuous yarn loops across the fabric in rows (can be done by hand). Very stretchy; **ladders** and unravels if a stitch breaks. E.g. jumpers, T-shirt jersey, socks, hats.
- **Warp knit**: many yarns loop vertically, **machine made** only. Less stretchy, more stable, **does not ladder or unravel**. E.g. swimwear, sportswear, lingerie, net/curtains, fleece base.
### Non-woven fabrics
- Made **directly from fibres** without spinning into yarn. **Do not fray**, cheap, but generally weaker, little drape, not stretchy.
- **Felt**: wool fibres matted together using **heat, moisture, pressure and friction** (agitation); scales on wool fibres lock together. Warm, does not fray, can be moulded; but weak, not washable, pills. E.g. hats, craft, snooker table covers, insulation.
- **Bonded fabric**: a web of fibres (often synthetic) held together by **adhesive**, by **heat** (melting thermoplastic fibres), or by needle-punching/stitching. Cheap, quick to make, no fray. E.g. **interfacing**, disposable face masks and medical gowns, cleaning cloths, tea bags, wet wipes.

## 6. Fabric finishes (brief)
- Finishes improve performance/appearance: **flame retardant** (children's nightwear, furniture), **stain resistant** / **water repellent** (coat, school uniform), **anti-static**, **easy care/crease resistant**, **anti-bacterial**, **brushing** (raises fibres for softness, e.g. brushed cotton/fleece).

## Misconception tags (use these exact ids)
- `fibre-yarn-fabric` — mixes up fibre, yarn and fabric (or the order)
- `natural-synthetic` — puts a fibre in the wrong family / thinks synthetic fibres come from plants
- `plant-animal` — mixes up plant (cellulose) and animal (protein) fibres or their sources
- `cotton-wool` — mixes up cotton and wool properties (warmth vs absorbency/coolness)
- `natural-mixup` — other mix-ups between cotton, linen, wool, silk
- `synthetic-mixup` — mixes up polyester, polyamide and elastane
- `elastane-alone` — thinks elastane is used on its own / doesn't know it's added in small amounts
- `blend-meaning` — confuses blend and mixture, or thinks blending only lowers cost
- `polycotton-props` — wrong properties for polycotton
- `warp-weft` — mixes up warp and weft directions
- `selvedge` — doesn't know what/where the selvedge is
- `weave-type` — mixes up plain, twill and satin weaves
- `knit-type` — mixes up weft and warp knitting (laddering, hand/machine)
- `woven-vs-knit` — wrong properties for woven vs knitted (stretch, fray)
- `nonwoven` — mixes up felt and bonded fabrics or thinks non-wovens are woven/knitted
- `synthetic-melt` — doesn't know synthetics melt/heat-set, or thinks they biodegrade
- `property-word` — uses the wrong property word (absorbent vs breathable, etc.)
