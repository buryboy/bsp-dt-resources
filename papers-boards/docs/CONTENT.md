# Papers & boards — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.6 Materials and their working properties: papers and boards)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth.

## 1. Where paper comes from
- Paper and board are made from **cellulose fibres**, mostly from **wood pulp** (mainly fast-growing **softwood** trees such as pine and spruce), a **renewable** resource if forests are replanted (managed forests; the **FSC** logo shows wood from responsibly managed forests).
- Steps: trees felled → **debarked** → **chipped** → **pulped** (wood chips are broken down into fibres, mechanically by grinding or chemically by cooking with chemicals, mixed with lots of water) → **bleached** (to make it white) and additives/dyes added → pulp sprayed onto a moving **wire mesh**; water drains away and fibres mat together → **pressed** between rollers → **dried** on heated rollers → **calendered** (polished between smooth rollers for a smooth finish) → wound onto huge reels, then cut to size.
- **Recycled paper**: waste paper is collected, sorted, pulped with water, de-inked and made into paper again. Each time, the fibres get **shorter and weaker**, so paper can only be recycled about **5–7 times**; recycled pulp is often mixed with new ("virgin") fibres. Recycling saves trees, energy and landfill space.
- Paper and board are **biodegradable** and **recyclable**. Downsides: making paper uses a lot of **water and energy**, and bleaching uses chemicals.

## 2. Weight, thickness and size
- Paper is graded by weight in **gsm = grams per square metre** (the mass of one 1 m × 1 m sheet). Normal printer/copier paper is about **80 gsm**.
- Above about **200 gsm** it is called **board** (card). Board thickness is measured in **microns**: **1000 microns = 1 mm** (so 500 microns = 0.5 mm).
- **A sizes**: **A0 has an area of 1 m²**. Each size is **half** the one before (fold/cut the long side in half): A0 → A1 → A2 → A3 → A4 → A5. **A4 = 210 × 297 mm; A3 = 297 × 420 mm** (two A4s side by side); A5 = 148 × 210 mm. The bigger the number, the smaller the sheet.
- Sums: an A0 sheet of 80 gsm paper weighs 80 g. A4 is 1/16 of A0, so one A4 sheet of 80 gsm weighs 80 ÷ 16 = 5 g. A ream (500 sheets) of 80 gsm A4 = 2500 g = 2.5 kg.

## 3. Papers (AQA list) — typical weights
| Paper | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Layout paper** (about 50 gsm) | thin, lightweight, smooth, **translucent** (you can see a drawing through it), cheap, takes pencil and marker | sketching and developing ideas; tracing through to refine a design |
| **Tracing paper** (about 60–90 gsm) | thin, **transparent/see-through**, hard, smooth, strong | copying and tracing drawings; overlays |
| **Bleed proof paper** (about 70 gsm) | smooth, hard, specially coated so **marker ink stays on the surface and does not spread (bleed) or soak through** | marker-pen rendering and presentation drawings |
| **Cartridge paper** (about 120–150 gsm) | thick, good-quality, opaque, **slightly textured** surface that grips pencil, crayon, pastel, ink and watercolour | drawing, sketching, painting |
| **Grid paper** (about 80 gsm) | printed with a faint grid of **squares or isometric** lines as a guide | scale and working drawings, isometric (3D) sketches, graphs, nets |

## 4. Boards (AQA list)
| Board | Make-up | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corrugated card** | a wavy **fluted** layer glued between two flat **liners** | strong and stiff for its weight, lightweight, **absorbs impacts**, insulates, cheap, recyclable | shipping/packaging boxes, pizza boxes, protective packaging |
| **Duplex board** | two layers; one side **white and coated for printing**, the other grey/brown; cheaper than solid white board | tough, lightweight, good print surface on one side | food packaging such as cereal boxes, frozen-food cartons |
| **Foil lined board** | board laminated with a thin layer of **aluminium foil** | **barrier against moisture and oxygen; reflects heat (keeps food hot)**; | takeaway and ready-meal container lids, drinks/juice cartons |
| **Foam core board** | **polystyrene foam** sandwiched between two sheets of card/paper | very lightweight, rigid, smooth, easy to cut with a craft knife; dents easily | architectural and design models, mounting photos and artwork, point-of-sale displays |
| **Ink jet card** | card treated/coated so ink-jet ink **dries on the surface without bleeding** | bright white, gives sharp, vivid printed images | printing photographs, presentation work, greetings cards on an ink-jet printer |
| **Solid white board** | top-quality **bleached** board, white all the way through | strong, stiff, smooth, **excellent print surface on both sides** | book covers, premium packaging (perfume, cosmetics, chocolates), greetings cards |
- **Laminating** (layering with another material) adds properties: foil (barrier), polythene film (waterproof: drinks cartons), or a plastic film for gloss.

## 5. Choosing
- Ask: does it need to be see-through? take marker without bleeding? be printed on one or both sides? protect something? keep food hot / liquid in? be very light and rigid for a model? cheap? recyclable?
