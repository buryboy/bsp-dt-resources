# Smart, modern & composite materials — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.3 Developments in new materials)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Students have done Material Properties, Metals, Polymers and Textiles. Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth.

## 1. The four groups
- **Modern materials**: materials developed through new or improved processes, e.g. as a result of human design (graphene, metal foams, titanium).
- **Smart materials**: materials whose physical properties **change in response to a change in their surroundings (an external stimulus)** such as **temperature, light, stress/force, moisture or pH**, and (usually) **change back** when the stimulus is removed. They are **reversible** and need **no electrical control**.
- **Composite materials**: made by combining **two or more different materials** to make a new material with **improved properties**. Usually a **reinforcement** (fibres/particles, gives strength) held in a **matrix** (binds and holds it in shape). The materials stay separate (not chemically mixed like an alloy).
- **Technical textiles**: textiles engineered for **performance/function** rather than appearance.

## 2. Modern materials
| Material | What it is | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Graphene** | a single layer of carbon atoms (one atom thick) arranged in a hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice | extremely **strong** (about 200 times stronger than steel by weight), extremely **lightweight**, **flexible**, almost **transparent**, excellent **conductor** of heat and electricity; expensive to make in large sheets | being developed for flexible touch screens, faster batteries and electronics, lightweight sports equipment (tennis racquets, bike frames), water filters, coatings |
| **Metal foams** | a metal (often **aluminium**) with lots of gas bubbles/pores trapped in it (made by injecting gas into molten metal or using a foaming agent) | very **lightweight** (low density, some float on water), **stiff and strong for its weight**, **absorbs impact energy** (crumples), good heat and sound insulation; recyclable | car crumple zones and bumper reinforcement, aerospace panels, sound-proofing, bone implants (titanium foam lets bone grow in), heat exchangers |
| **Titanium** | a metal (non-ferrous); usually used as an alloy | very **strong**, **lightweight** (about half the density of steel), **excellent corrosion resistance**, **biocompatible** (not rejected by the body), high melting point; **expensive** and hard to process | hip and knee replacements, dental implants, aircraft and spacecraft parts, racing bikes, glasses frames, watches, jewellery |

## 3. Smart materials
| Material | Stimulus → change | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape memory alloy (SMA)**, e.g. **nitinol** (nickel + titanium) | can be bent/deformed when cool; when **heated** above its transition temperature it **returns to its original "remembered" shape**. Some types are **superelastic**: bend a long way and spring back. It can be "programmed" by heating to a high temperature while held in a shape | glasses frames that spring back if sat on, dental braces/orthodontic wire (warmth of mouth pulls teeth), stents (opens up in a blood vessel), actuators (a wire that shortens when an electric current heats it), fire sprinkler/safety valves, anti-scald valves in showers |
| **Thermochromic pigment** | **colour changes with temperature** (heat); changes back when it cools | kettles and baby spoons/bath toys that show if too hot, battery testers, forehead thermometer strips, mugs and novelty clothing, food packaging that shows when a drink is cold enough |
| **Photochromic pigment** | **colour changes with light** (UV/sunlight): darkens in bright light, clears when the light goes | lenses in sunglasses/glasses that darken outside (reactions lenses), T-shirts and nail varnish that change colour in sunlight, security printing, window glass |
- (Mention only) other smart materials: piezoelectric (gives a small voltage when squeezed, e.g. gas lighter, or vibrates when voltage applied, e.g. buzzer), quantum tunnelling composite (QTC: insulator until squeezed), hydrogels (absorb water and swell).
- **Not smart**: something that changes only because it is powered or controlled (e.g. an LED changing colour), or a change that does not reverse (a material that just melts or burns).

## 4. Composite materials
| Composite | Matrix + reinforcement | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Concrete** | **cement** (matrix, binds) + **aggregate** (sand and gravel/stones) + **water**, which sets hard by a chemical reaction | very **strong in compression** (squashing) but **weak in tension** (pulling); cheap, durable, fire-resistant, can be cast into any shape. **Reinforced concrete** has **steel bars (rebar)** cast inside to carry the tension | buildings, bridges, foundations, roads, pavements, dams |
| **Glass reinforced plastic (GRP)**, fibreglass | **polyester resin** (matrix) + **glass fibres** (reinforcement) | strong, lightweight, can be moulded into large complex shapes, does not rot or corrode, waterproof; cheaper than CFRP | boat hulls, canoes, car and lorry body panels, garden ponds, helmets, water tanks, slides |
| **Carbon fibre reinforced plastic (CFRP)** | **epoxy resin** (matrix) + **carbon fibres** (reinforcement), often as woven carbon cloth | **extremely strong and stiff for its weight** (stronger than steel, much lighter), very lightweight; **expensive**; brittle impact failure; hard to recycle | Formula 1 car bodies, racing bikes, aircraft parts, tennis racquets, fishing rods, high-end sports equipment, prosthetic running blades |
- Other composites to mention: **plywood** and **MDF** are composites; **straw + mud** bricks (an early composite).

## 5. Technical textiles
| Textile | What it does | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Conductive fabrics** | contain conductive fibres or threads (e.g. **silver**, **copper**, **carbon**/stainless steel) so electricity can flow through the fabric | gloves that work on touchscreens, heated clothing, wearable electronics with sensors (heart-rate shirts), light-up clothing |
| **Fire resistant fabrics** | made from fibres that are inherently flame resistant (e.g. **Nomex**, an aramid) or treated with a flame retardant finish; do not catch fire easily, do not melt or drip, self-extinguish | firefighters' suits, racing drivers' overalls, oven gloves, furniture and theatre curtains, children's nightwear (finish) |
| **Kevlar** | an **aramid** synthetic fibre: very **high tensile strength** (about 5 times stronger than steel by weight), lightweight, **cut- and puncture-resistant**, flexible, heat resistant; damaged by UV | **bullet-proof (ballistic) vests**, cut-resistant gloves, helmets, motorcycle clothing, sails, ropes and tyres |
| **Microfibres with micro-encapsulation** | **microfibres** are ultra-fine synthetic fibres (finer than silk) making soft, light, breathable, quick-drying fabrics; **micro-encapsulation** traps tiny capsules of a substance (scent, moisturiser, insect repellent, antibacterial, vitamins) on the fibres that **burst slowly with wear/friction**, releasing it over time | scented or moisturising clothing, antibacterial sports socks, insect-repellent clothing, medical dressings, cleaning cloths (microfibre) |

## Misconception tags (use these exact ids)
- `smart-meaning` — thinks smart = electronic/powered, or doesn't know smart changes are reversible responses to a stimulus
- `stimulus-mixup` — wrong stimulus (thermochromic ↔ light, photochromic ↔ heat, SMA ↔ light)
- `sma-mixup` — doesn't understand shape memory (needs heat to recover, superelastic, nitinol make-up)
- `composite-meaning` — confuses composites with alloys/mixtures/blends, or doesn't know matrix vs reinforcement
- `matrix-reinforcement` — swaps matrix and reinforcement
- `concrete-tension` — thinks concrete is strong in tension / doesn't know why steel rebar is used
- `gfrp-cfrp` — mixes up GRP and CFRP (cost, strength, uses, resin)
- `modern-mixup` — mixes up graphene, metal foam and titanium properties/uses
- `graphene-structure` — wrong idea of what graphene is (metal, thick, plastic)
- `metal-foam` — doesn't know why foam absorbs impact / is light
- `titanium-props` — thinks titanium is heavy, rusts or is cheap
- `kevlar-props` — wrong properties/uses of Kevlar
- `tech-textile-mixup` — mixes up conductive, fire resistant, Kevlar and microencapsulated fabrics
- `microencap` — doesn't know how micro-encapsulation releases its substance
- `group-mixup` — puts a material in the wrong group (modern / smart / composite / technical textile)
