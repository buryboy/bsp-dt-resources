# Metals & alloys — content sheet (AQA GCSE D&T 8552, 3.1.6 Materials and their working properties: metals and alloys)

Year 10, one-hour lesson. Students have done the Material Properties workbook (know: strength, hardness, toughness, malleability, ductility, elasticity, density, conductivity, corrosion). Every fact on a page must come from this sheet (or be plainly consistent with it). GCSE D&T depth.

## 1. Where metals come from
- Metals are found in the Earth's crust as **ores** (rock containing a metal compound). Ore is **mined** (or quarried), then the metal is **extracted** and **refined**.
- **Iron** comes from **iron ore** (haematite). It is heated in a **blast furnace** with **coke** (carbon) and **limestone**; hot air is blasted in; molten iron ("pig iron") runs out at the bottom; impurities float off as **slag**. Iron is then turned into **steel** by removing most of the carbon (blowing oxygen through it) .
- **Aluminium** comes from **bauxite**. It is extracted by **electrolysis**, which uses a huge amount of **electricity** (expensive).
- **Copper** comes from copper ore; it is smelted and then purified by electrolysis.
- Metals are a **finite (non-renewable)** resource. Mining scars the landscape, destroys habitats, and extraction uses lots of energy and releases CO2.
- Metals can be **recycled** again and again without losing quality. Recycling aluminium saves about **95%** of the energy needed to make it from bauxite. Steel is separated for recycling with magnets.

## 2. Three families
- **Ferrous metals contain iron.** Most are **magnetic** and will **rust** (corrode) when exposed to air and water.
- **Non-ferrous metals contain no iron.** They are **not magnetic** and **do not rust** (some tarnish/oxidise on the surface).
- **Alloys are a mixture of two or more elements, at least one a metal**, made to get improved properties (harder, stronger, more corrosion-resistant, lower melting point…). Alloys can be ferrous (contain iron) or non-ferrous.
- Pure metals are often too soft; alloying disrupts the regular layers of atoms so they cannot slide as easily → harder and stronger.

## 3. Ferrous metals (AQA list)
| Metal | Make-up | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low carbon steel (mild steel)** | iron + up to about 0.3% carbon | tough, ductile, malleable, easy to cut, bend, weld and machine; cheap; rusts easily | car bodies, nuts, bolts, screws, nails, girders, bike frames, school workshop projects |
| **High carbon steel (tool steel)** | iron + about 0.8–1.5% carbon | very hard, strong, keeps a sharp edge; less ductile, more brittle; can be hardened and tempered; rusts | chisels, files, saw blades, hammers, drill bits, knives, springs |
| **Cast iron** | iron + about 3.5% carbon | hard, very strong in compression, brittle (cracks if dropped/hit); melts and pours well so good for casting complex shapes; cheap; rusts | vices, manhole/drain covers, engine blocks, brake discs, cast-iron pans, radiators |
- **The carbon rule: more carbon → harder (and stronger), but less ductile/malleable and more brittle.**

## 4. Non-ferrous metals (AQA list)
| Metal | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Aluminium** | lightweight (low density, about a third of steel), soft, malleable, ductile, good conductor of heat and electricity, corrosion-resistant (a thin oxide layer protects it), silvery; melts 660 °C | drinks cans, kitchen foil, aircraft bodies, window frames, bike frames, saucepans, overhead power cables |
| **Copper** | excellent conductor of electricity and heat, very ductile and malleable, corrosion-resistant (slowly turns green: a patina), reddish-brown; melts 1083 °C | electrical wire and cables, water pipes, printed circuit boards, saucepan bases, roofing |
| **Tin** | soft, weak, very low melting point (232 °C), corrosion-resistant, non-toxic, bright silvery | thin coating on steel food cans ("tin cans" are tinplate), solder (with other metals) |
| **Zinc** | corrosion-resistant, low melting point (420 °C), fairly weak and brittle, bluish-grey | **galvanising** steel (zinc coating: buckets, gates, crash barriers, wheelbarrows), die-cast parts, batteries |
- Typical densities (g/cm³): aluminium 2.7, zinc 7.1, tin 7.3, steel 7.8, brass 8.5, copper 8.9.

## 5. Alloys (AQA list)
| Alloy | Made from | Properties | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brass** (non-ferrous alloy) | copper + zinc (about 65% / 35%) | harder than copper, corrosion-resistant, golden yellow, casts and machines well, good conductor | taps and plumbing fittings, door handles, locks and keys, musical instruments, pins of electrical plugs |
| **Stainless steel** (ferrous alloy) | steel + chromium (about 18%) + nickel (about 8%) | hard, tough, strong, **resists corrosion (does not rust)**, shiny, hygienic; hard to cut and shape; expensive | cutlery, kitchen sinks, saucepans, surgical instruments, kitchen equipment |
| **High speed steel (HSS)** (ferrous alloy) | steel + tungsten, chromium, vanadium | very hard, wear-resistant, **stays hard even when red hot** from friction | drill bits, lathe cutting tools, milling cutters, router bits |
- Chromium in stainless steel forms an invisible protective oxide layer.

## 6. Rusting and protecting
- **Rust** forms when iron/steel meets **both oxygen (air) and water**. Take either away and it does not rust. Salt speeds it up.
- Protection: a **barrier** (paint, oil/grease, plastic dip coating, powder coating), **plating** (chromium, tin), or **galvanising** (zinc coating; zinc protects the steel even if scratched). Or choose a metal that does not rust (stainless steel, aluminium, brass).

## 7. Stock forms (brief)
- Metals are bought in standard **stock forms**: sheet, plate, flat bar/strip, round bar (rod), square bar, hexagonal bar, tube (round/square), angle, wire. Standard sizes keep cost down.

## Misconception tags (use these exact ids)
- `ferrous-meaning` — unsure that ferrous = contains iron; thinks all metals are magnetic or all rust
- `alloy-meaning` — thinks an alloy is a pure metal, a coating, or a compound formed by reaction
- `alloy-contents` — wrong ingredients (e.g. brass = copper + tin; stainless = steel + zinc)
- `carbon-rule` — thinks more carbon makes steel softer/more ductile
- `steel-mixup` — mixes up low carbon steel, high carbon steel and cast iron properties/uses
- `nonferrous-mixup` — mixes up aluminium, copper, tin, zinc properties/uses
- `alloy-mixup` — mixes up brass, stainless steel, high speed steel properties/uses
- `stainless-family` — thinks stainless steel is non-ferrous / a pure metal
- `rust-conditions` — thinks rust needs only water or only air; or that all metals rust
- `galvanise-meaning` — mixes up galvanising with alloying/plating with tin/painting
- `tin-can` — thinks food cans are solid tin / aluminium foil is tin
- `ore-extraction` — mixes up blast furnace and electrolysis, or iron ore and bauxite
- `finite-recycle` — thinks metals are renewable, or lose quality when recycled
- `property-word` — uses the wrong property word (hard vs tough, ductile vs malleable)
